0.2 hRound 1c — decision
Hotspot class: memory-bound glue between the GEMMs, specifically the FP8 operand layout copies.
Change: nanochat/fp8.py gets a Triton "dual" quantization kernel; _Float8Matmul quantizes each operand
once into both [M,N] and [N,M] layouts, saves the transposed copies, and the backward's three transposing
copies (go_T, in_col, w_col) disappear (each becomes a free .t() view of column-major, exactly the
layout _scaled_mm requires). Target tier N0 (bitwise).
0.6 hRound 2 — Triton dual-layout FP8 quantization (nanochat/fp8.py)
Change: _Float8Matmul quantizes each reduced-precision operand (bf16 activations, bf16 gradients)
with one Triton kernel that writes BOTH memory layouts the three GEMMs need:
  - out  = [M, N] row-major (the forward GEMM's A operand, the backward's grad_output),
  - outT = [N, M] row-major (a free .t() view of the column-major B operand).
The backward's go_T = go_fp8.t().contiguous() and in_col = _to_col_major(in_fp8) disappear;
w_col (the fp32 master weight, 4x smaller than one micro-batch of activations) keeps the
reference copy. The amax/scale/inv_scale are still the reference's own expressions (so Inductor keeps
fusing the amax into the kernel that produces the operand), and the kernel reproduces
x.float()*scale -> clamp -> fp8 cast exactly (verified bitwise over dtypes x shapes x magnitudes).
Switched by NANOCHAT_FP8_QUANT ("ref" | "dual", default "dual").

1.0 hRound 3 result — fused RMSNorm REVERTED (net loss)
time (dual-layout FP8 + coordesc + fused RMSNorm): speedup 1.0238 vs 1.0597 without the fused norm.
So replacing F.rms_norm with standalone Triton kernels LOSES ~3.4% even though the standalone kernels are
3x faster in isolation (51 µs vs 151 µs for 200 MB). Cause: Inductor's fused kernels were carrying
neighboring work for free — the block norm is fused with the amax of the next FP8 operand, and the QK norm
is fused with RoPE and the 1.2 scale, so the norm's output never goes to memory twice. My version adds a
separate amax pass over the norm output and a second read/write of q and k for the RoPE, which costs more
than the kernel speedup. Reverted (nanochat/rmsnorm.py deleted, gpt.py back to F.rms_norm); the lesson is
recorded: a faster kernel that breaks a fusion can be a net loss — measure end to end.

2.7 hRound 6 — fused MLP cell: ABANDONED (Triton transposed-store bug)
The MLP backward chain (relu^2 derivative -> e5m2 quantization in both layouts) costs 713 us per block
in the reference (Inductor: [relu^2 bwd + amax] 309 us + materialize the derivative 214 us + the
dual-layout cast 190 us). Two Triton kernels that compute the derivative from (grad, pre-activation)
directly - one for the amax, one for the quantized pair - measure 498 us at 4.2/4.0 TB/s, i.e. a 215 us
per block saving (20.6 ms/update, ~2.2%).
BUT: a kernel that loads TWO tensors and writes both layouts writes a WRONG transposed output (37% of
the bytes differ from its own row-major output), with either tl.trans(o) or transposed index math, with
or without a conditional store. The single-load _fp8_dual_kernel is correct in both layouts, so this is
a Triton codegen issue with the two-load + dual-store pattern. Reverted (nanochat/fp8.py, gpt.py back to
commit ce429f7); the kernels and the measurements are kept in this log. The submission is the validated
1.0715 version.
3.0 hRound 7 — share the attention input quantization: ABANDONED (Inductor kernel fails to compile)
c_q, c_k and c_v take the same tensor, so the reference quantizes it three times (three dual-layout
kernels over the same 100 MB). Quantizing once and handing all three GEMMs the same operand
(_QuantizeOnce + _Float8MatmulPreQ) measured 1.1481 in time - but that version had a bug
(the shared quantization returned None from its backward, so the attention input norm lost its
gradient and autograd skipped part of the backward: the loss at step 1 diverged from the reference by

is the validated 1.0715 version again.

5.2 hRound 10 — Triton dual-store bug characterized; MLP cell abandoned again
The two-load + dual-store bug is now isolated precisely (all with bf16 operands, fp8 output, tile 128x128):
  - 1 load + 2 stores: correct.
  - 2 loads + 2 stores: the transposed bytes come out permuted (row-major store is correct).
  - 2 loads + 1 store (either one alone): correct.
  - A reduction barrier (v = o.to(f32) + tl.sum(o.to(f32), axis=1, keep_dims=True) * 0.0) before the
    transposed store fixes it for a plain elementwise value (cost ~1%), but NOT once the value comes from a
    relu (tl.where, tl.maximum, (h+|h|)/2, h-min(h,0) all re-break it) - so the MLP's fused
    derivative cannot use a single kernel. Splitting it into three kernels (amax, row-major cast, transpose)
    would save only 53 us of 713 us per block (0.5%), which does not justify the restructure. Reverted;
    the submission stays at the validated 1.0748.

7.2 hRound 13 — fused [norm + amax] with the amax threaded into the quantization: net loss, reverted
The block norms' output is exactly the operand of the FP8 GEMM that consumes it, so the amax the
quantization needs can be produced by the norm itself. nanochat::norm_amax (a Triton kernel that writes
the normalized tensor and one partial max per program, plus part.max()) plus a closed-form rms_norm
backward, with the amax threaded through Float8Linear.forward -> _Float8Matmul -> _to_fp8_dual
(the amax passthrough is bitwise identical to the self-computed one). Verified against F.rms_norm:
forward within 1 bf16 ULP, gradient 3e-8 relative L2 in fp32 / 1e-5 in bf16, all shapes and dtypes.
Measured 1.0520 vs 1.0748 for the same tree without it - a 2% net LOSS, the second time a norm
replacement loses end to end (round 3 was the first). The fused Inductor kernels win because the extra
custom-op launches, the separate partial-max reduction and the lost fusions cost more than the kernel
speedup. Reverted; the submission is the validated 1.0748 version.

7.6 hRound 16 — the update boundary and CUDA graphs: no lever
Gap analysis of a fresh profile: the GPU is 99.5% busy inside the profiled span (8.0 ms of inter-kernel
gaps over 2 updates, the largest 1.49 ms at the train_loss.item() sync). The measured per-update gap
between the wall time (~0.938 s) and the kernel time (0.9198 s) is ~18 ms and sits at the update
boundary: the judge's post-step hook synchronizes, then the training loop does zero_grad,
train_loss.item() (a hard sync), synchronize(), the f-string logging and the loop prologue before
the next micro-batch is queued. The host dispatch of the compiled forward/backward (the AOT prologue,
the autograd engine, 4 x ~70 ms of CompiledFunctionBackward CPU time) totals ~1.9 s per update against
0.92 s of GPU work, so the host is the marginal resource at the boundary.
- Deferring the .item() read past the logging would recover only ~2-3 ms (0.3%) and would make the
  logged loss lag by one step: not worth it.
- NANOCHAT_COMPILE_MODE=reduce-overhead (CUDA graphs, which would remove most of the host dispatch
  cost): SIGSEGV on both ranks with the FP8 custom ops in the graph. Rejected.
The submission is unchanged at the validated 1.0748.

9.1 hRound 18 — fused MLP cell, bitwise-identical but a net loss: reverted
The relu^2 derivative chain of the MLP backward (c_fc's grad_output) was re-implemented as three Triton
kerns inside one _MLPCell autograd function: an atomic amax over 2*relu(h)*g, a row-major e5m2 cast
with the scale derived in-kernel, and a transpose - the split avoids the Triton two-load dual-store bug.
With the derivative rounded to bf16 exactly where the reference materializes it, the cell is **bitwise
identical to the reference MLP path** (out, dx, both weight gradients: relL2 = 0; the quantized gradient
and its inverse scale match byte for byte).
Microbenchmark: 286 us per block against the reference chain's ~711 us. End to end: 1.0668 vs 1.0748
(a 0.7% loss) and the profile confirms +11.3 ms/update. Cause: the amax kernel issues 12288 atomics on a
single address per call; that costs 99 us in isolation but 197 us inside the model (contention), and the
cast and transpose are similarly ~2x slower in-model than in the microbenchmark, so the 3 kernels cost
53.8 ms/update against the 50 ms they replace. Lesson recorded: microbenchmark timings of the glue
kernels overestimate the in-model gain by ~2x.
Reverted; the submission is the validated 1.0748 version.
9.1 hRound 18 post-mortem (why the MLP cell lost, corrected)
The reference's chain is ONE Inductor kernel `triton_red_fused__to_copy_abs_max_mul_pow_relu_threshold_
backward_view` (307 us) that computes the relu^2 derivative, its amax AND materializes the bf16
derivative, followed by the dual-layout quantization (190 us) - 497 us per block at ~2.9 TB/s, not the
711 us the microbenchmark assumed. The cell's three kernels cost 560 us in-model (the atomic amax alone
197 us against 99 us in isolation), so the cell was 6 ms/update slower on the kernels it replaced, plus
the NCCL/GEMM drift of that run. Conclusion stands: reverted.
10.0 hRound 19 — `TORCHINDUCTOR_MULTI_KERNEL=1`: +0.3%, reproducible
Inductor's multi_kernel option (default 0) makes the compiler *tune* between the persistent and the
split-reduction sub-kernels per reduction instead of using the heuristic choice - relevant because the
FP8 amax reductions are full-tensor reductions with a single output, where the codegen choice matters.
Two time runs: 1.0778 and 1.0776 (windows [0.9372, 0.9444, 0.9415] and [0.9343, 0.9428, 0.9466])
against 1.0735/1.0743/1.0748 for the same tree without it - a reproducible +0.3%. Cost: the cold
compile grows from ~880 s to ~1135 s (the run is 1138 s, inside the 2700 s timeout with a 2.4x margin).
Also tested and rejected in the same family: TORCHINDUCTOR_COOPERATIVE_REDUCTIONS=1 (1.0034 - much
worse: cross-block sync does not pay for these shapes). A full check of the multi-kernel setting is
running next.
