### Failed direction: selective gradient checkpointing (the last layers keep their activations)
Idea: the layers that are not checkpointed are not recomputed during the backward, which is a
whole extra forward of the model.  With the last 8-12 layers un-checkpointed the timing run went
from 1757 ms to 1622 ms/step (1.7027x) and the peak memory rose from 74.25 to 98.62 GiB.
The implementation hooks transformers' _set_gradient_checkpointing and lets the last layers of
the LLaMA run without the checkpoint (llava/selective_ckpt.py, switch LLAVA_CKPT_SKIP_LAST).

It deadlocks: the run hangs in the ZeRO-3 backward, both ranks spinning in a NCCL collective
(py-spy: the autograd worker thread of both ranks is busy inside the backward; the GPU is at 100%
but no step finishes).  Two runs hung the same way (the first one with a version whose counter was
also counting the vision tower's layers, the second one with the counter fixed to count only the
LLaMA decoder layers).  The same tree without the switch runs fine, and an earlier variant of the
idea (which un-checkpointed the *first* layers, because of the same counter bug) did finish 24
steps, so the deadlock is not deterministic - it smells like the ZeRO-3 gradient reduction being
issued in a different order on the two ranks when the order in which the layers' gradients become
ready changes.  Not worth the risk: the change is left out of the tree (llava/selective_ckpt.py
is not imported by anything).

4.1 hRound 14 (investigation) - the copies of the gradients into the ipg bucket
The profile of the tree of round 13 (/tmp/r14/profile) showed 30 ms more copies of the gradients
of the MLP into the ipg bucket of DeepSpeed than the tree of round 11 (96 extra copies of 45.1M
elements per step).  The instrumentation of DeepSpeedZeroOptimizer_Stage3.__add_grad_to_ipg_bucket
(a copy of the tree in /tmp/dbg/llava, LLAVA_DEBUG_IPG) shows that *every* parameter is added to
the ipg bucket twice, with and without the cache of the projections: it is a behaviour of
DeepSpeed (the hook on the accumulation of the gradient fires again when the gradient of the
parameter is accumulated a second time) and not something the cache introduces.  Making the
gradient of the weight contiguous (.contiguous()) changes nothing (1460 ms instead of 1457 ms),
so the change was reverted.

4.7 hRound 19 - fused SwiGLU, LLAVA_FUSED_SWIGLU=1 (23:40-23:50 UTC)
Profile finding that drove it (/tmp/r17/profile/profile_tree.md): the elementwise operations of the
MLP are the biggest remaining item that is not a matrix multiplication: the SiLU (13.7 ms), the
multiplication of the SiLU and the up projection (28.4 ms of the multiplications of the step) and
the SiLU of the backward (9.0 ms).  The chain reads and writes the two big tensors of the layer
(the gate and the up projection, 13600 x 11008) four times.

Change: llava/fused_swiglu.py computes silu(gate) * up in one Triton kernel and its backward
in one more, and the MLP of the LLaMA layers uses it (switch LLAVA_FUSED_SWIGLU).  The
arithmetic is the one of PyTorch, with the rounding of every intermediate result kept: the SiLU
is x / (1 + exp(-x)) in fp32, rounded to bf16, the product is rounded to bf16, and the backward
is d_up = d_out * silu(gate), d_silu = d_out * up (rounded to bf16) and
d_gate = d_silu * s * (1 + gate * (1 - s)) with s = 1 / (1 + exp(-gate)).

Two details of Triton matter for the exactness: tl.exp is the fast ex2.approx and differs
from the expf of PyTorch (48% of the values), so the kernel uses tl.math.exp (the libdevice
__nv_expf); and the division of the sigmoid uses tl.math.div_rn.

Numerics: tier N0.  Checked on the real shapes (13600 x 11008) on the GPU: the result and the
two gradients are bitwise identical to the ones of PyTorch (0 differing elements out of 149.7M).
