8.9 hRound 87 — handoff note: what a future attempt would need (02:00)
For anyone continuing this work, the measured facts that constrain it:

1. The wins are in work removal, not kernel speed. Every kernel in the final step is at its roofline or at a
   library limit; the eight gains came from *doing less* (fewer micro-steps of overhead, one all-reduce instead of
   74, baddbmm instead of a separate elementwise pass, no relu(h) buffer, one fused dual-layout store instead of
   two kernels).
2. Owning a region only pays when Inductor's own kernel there is pathological (uncoalesced transposed stores,
   as in the MLP gradient path) *and* the region has no producer fusion worth keeping. Four rewrites that broke a
   producer fusion (rounds 74, 75, 81, 82) all lost 4-10 ms, with correct numerics.
3. The remaining ~20 ms of sub-roofline time is inside fused norm/rope backward graphs. Capturing it needs a
   custom Function spanning several autograd nodes with the *forward* left traceable; every attempt so far lost
   more on the forward than it gained on the backward. A future attempt should first measure the forward's fusion
   cost in isolation (e.g. profile the Function with its backward stubbed) before writing kernels.
4. The hardware is the binding constraint: 693 W of a 700 W cap, SM clock 1980 -> ~1440 MHz during a run, so
   time per update ~ energy per update / cap. Faster kernels that draw more power mostly buy clock reduction.
5. Score arithmetic: the harness divides min(refA, refB) per window (1.0162/1.0210/1.0176 from
   runs/bootstrap/baseline) by the candidate's windows and takes the minimum. Improving the *slowest* candidate
   window is what moves the score; the per-window spread here is thermal (~0.4%).
